Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Introduction

Some nouns you can count (one apple, two apples). Some you can't (water, money, time). This changes the words you use with them, like many, much and a lot of.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Know the difference between countable and uncountable nouns.
  • Use many with countable nouns and much with uncountable nouns.
  • Use a lot of with both.

1. Countable and Uncountable

Form: countable: a / an + noun or noun + s / uncountable: no a / an, no -s

When to use it: Countable nouns are things you can count one by one. Uncountable nouns are things you can't.

  1. I eat an apple every day.
  2. She has two children.
  3. I don't drink milk.

Common uncountable nouns: water, milk, coffee, bread, rice, money, time, work, information, advice, furniture, luggage.

2. Much and Many

Form: many + plural noun / much + uncountable noun

When to use it: Use them mostly in negatives and questions.

  1. How many people work in your office?
  2. I don't have much time today.
  3. Are there many tourists in summer?

How much? is also how you ask the price: How much is this?

3. A Lot Of

Form: a lot of + plural noun or uncountable noun

When to use it: Use it in positive sentences, with both types of noun.

  1. She has a lot of friends.
  2. We drink a lot of coffee at work.
  3. There's a lot of traffic in the morning.

I have many friends is correct, but it sounds formal. In everyday English, say a lot of.

Summary: Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Countable Uncountable
Examples apple, friend, chair water, money, time
a / an a friend, an apple ✗ (not a water)
Plural friends, apples ✗ (not moneys)
Negatives and questions many: How many friends? much: How much money?
Positive a lot of friends a lot of money
