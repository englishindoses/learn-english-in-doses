Will

Introduction

Use will to say what you think will happen, and when you decide something at the moment you're speaking. In speaking, will is usually short: I'll, you'll, it'll.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Make predictions with will.
  • Use I'll for quick decisions and offers.
  • Make negatives with won't and questions with Will...?

1. Predictions

Form: subject + will ('ll) + base verb

When to use it: Use it to say what you think will happen.

  1. I think it will be sunny tomorrow.
  2. You'll love this film.
  3. She'll be a great teacher.

I think... + will is very common: I think you'll like it.

2. Quick Decisions and Offers

Form: I'll + base verb

When to use it: Use it when you decide something now, or offer to help.

  1. I'll have the chicken, please.
  2. It's cold. I'll close the window.
  3. Your bags look heavy. I'll help you.

Plans you made before use going to. Decisions you make now use will.

3. Negatives and Questions

Form: won't (will not) + base verb / Will + subject + base verb...?

When to use it: Use won't for negatives and put will first in questions.

  1. Don't worry. I won't be late.
  2. It won't take long.
  3. Will you be home for dinner?

Summary: Will

Use Form Example
Prediction will + verb I think you'll like it.
Quick decision I'll + verb I'll have a tea, please.
Offer I'll + verb I'll carry that for you.
Negative won't + verb I won't be late.
Question Will + subject + verb? Will you be home?
