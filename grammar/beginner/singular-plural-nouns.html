These, Those and Plural Nouns

Introduction

When there is more than one thing, English usually adds -s to the noun. Some common nouns are different, and you need to learn them. Use these and those with plural nouns.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Make plural nouns with -s and -es.
  • Use common irregular plurals, like children and people.
  • Use these and those with plural nouns.

1. Regular Plurals

Form: noun + -s / noun + -es (after s, sh, ch, x) / consonant + y → -ies

When to use it: Use the plural when there is more than one.

  1. I have two sisters.
  2. We need three boxes.
  3. I love old cities.

Also: some nouns ending in -f or -fe change to -ves: knife → knives, wife → wives.

2. Irregular Plurals

Form: man → men, woman → women, child → children, person → people, foot → feet, tooth → teeth

When to use it: These nouns don't add -s. Learn them one by one.

  1. My children go to the same school.
  2. Lots of people work from home now.
  3. My feet hurt.

3. These and Those

Form: these + plural noun (near) / those + plural noun (far)

When to use it: These is the plural of this. Those is the plural of that.

  1. These shoes are new.
  2. Are those your keys?
  3. I love these photos.

Summary: These, Those and Plural Nouns

Rule Singular Plural
Most nouns: + s car cars
After s, sh, ch, x: + es bus, watch buses, watches
Consonant + y: -ies city cities
Some -f / -fe: -ves knife knives
Irregular child, person, man, woman children, people, men, women
Near this bag these bags
Far that bag those bags
