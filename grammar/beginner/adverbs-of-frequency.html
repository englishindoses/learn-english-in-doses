Adverbs of Frequency

Introduction

Adverbs of frequency tell us how often something happens. You use them all the time when you talk about your routine.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use the six most common adverbs of frequency, from always to never.
  • Put the adverb in the right place with most verbs.
  • Put the adverb in the right place with the verb be.

1. The Adverbs

  • always = every time (100%)
  • usually = almost every time (90%)
  • often = many times (70%)
  • sometimes = some of the time (50%)
  • rarely = not often (10%)
  • never = not at any time (0%)

When to use it: Use these words to say how often you do something.

  1. I always have coffee in the morning.
  2. She sometimes works from home.
  3. We never eat meat.

2. Word Order

Form: subject + adverb + verb

When to use it: The adverb goes before the main verb.

  1. He usually gets up at seven.
  2. They often visit their grandparents at the weekend.
  3. I rarely take the bus to work.

3. Word Order with Be

Form: subject + am / is / are + adverb

When to use it: The adverb goes after am, is and are.

  1. I'm always tired on Mondays.
  2. The train is often late.
  3. She's never at home at lunchtime.

Summary: Adverbs of Frequency

Adverb How often With most verbs With be
always 100% I always have breakfast. I'm always tired on Mondays.
usually 90% She usually drives to work. She's usually at home in the evening.
often 70% We often go to the cinema. The train is often late.
sometimes 50% He sometimes works late. He's sometimes busy at weekends.
rarely 10% I rarely watch TV. I'm rarely ill.
never 0% They never eat fast food. They're never late.

Choose the correct answer for each question.

2. Which sentence is correct?

A. She always is late.
B. She is always late.

B. usually
