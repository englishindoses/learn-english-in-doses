Possessive 's

Introduction

Add 's to a person to show that something belongs to them: Tom's car, my sister's name. It's very common when you talk about family, friends and work.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use 's to say who something belongs to.
  • Use s' with plural nouns like parents and friends.
  • Know when 's means is.

1. Person + 's

Form: person + 's + noun

When to use it: Use it to say who something belongs to.

  1. This is Tom's car.
  2. My sister's name is Emma.
  3. My manager's office is upstairs.

2. Plural Nouns + '

Form: plural noun ending in -s + ' + noun

When to use it: When the noun is plural and ends in -s, add only an apostrophe.

  1. My parents' house is near the sea.
  2. The students' books are on the table.
  3. My friends' kids are really funny.

Irregular plurals add 's: the children's toys, the women's team.

3. 's = Is or Possessive?

Form: name + 's + noun = possessive / name + 's + adjective or place = is

When to use it: Look at the word after 's to know what it means.

  1. Tom's car is red. (= the car of Tom)
  2. Tom's at work. (= Tom is at work)
  3. Sarah's tired. (= Sarah is tired)

Summary: Possessive 's

Rule Example
One person: + 's Tom's phone, my sister's car
Plural ending in -s: + ' my parents' house, the students' books
Irregular plural: + 's the children's room, the women's team
's = is Tom's at work. (= Tom is at work)
