Modal Verbs: can, must, should

We use modal verbs to talk about ability (can), rules (must) and advice (should).

By the end of this lesson you can:

  1. Use can / can't for ability and requests.
  2. Use must / mustn't for rules.
  3. Use should / shouldn't to give advice.

One big rule runs through the whole lesson. Watch for it in the Warm Up.

Warm Up

Write can or can't.

Fish swim.
Penguins fly.
A baby drive a car.
Cats see in the dark.
You see the sun at night.
Parrots talk.
A snake walk.
Most people ride a bike.
You got 0 out of 8.

Look at the verb after can in every sentence. Is there a to? Is there an -s? Tell your teacher the rule.

can and can't

subjectcan / can'tbase verb…
I / You / He / She / We / Theycanswim.
My brothercan'tdrivea car.

Question: Can you help me?   Short answers: Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

can is the same for every subject. No -s, ever: She cans / She can swims.

The key point: after can, use the base verb. No to.
I can't to drive. → I can't drive.
Do you can help me? → Can you help me?

must and mustn't

subjectmust / mustn'tbase verb…
Youmustweara seatbelt.
Passengersmustn'tsmokeon the plane.

must = this is a rule, or it is very important.   mustn't = don't do it!

Same pattern as can: one form for every subject, no -s, no to.

The key point: after must and mustn't, use the base verb.
You must to wear a seatbelt. → You must wear a seatbelt.

should and shouldn't

(wh- word)subject / shouldbase verb…

Whatshould Ido?

should = it's a good idea.   shouldn't = it's a bad idea. We use them to give advice.

Back to the big rule: should works like can and must. Base verb, no to, no -s.
You should to see a doctor. → You should see a doctor.

Speaking: interview your teacher

Ask your teacher these questions. After every answer, ask one more question of your own.

  1. Can you play a musical instrument?
  2. How many languages can you speak?
  3. Can you remember your first day at work?
  4. What must people do in your office?
  5. What must you never do in your job?
  6. What can't you cook?
  7. What should I visit in your city?
  8. What should I do to improve my English?

Speaking: answer the question

Choose a topic, then click the button. Answer in two or three sentences, not one word.

#

While you speak

  • Use the modal in your answer: Yes, I can. / You should…
  • Add a reason: … because it's dangerous.
  • Ask back: And you? What can you do?
  • Careful: after can, must and should, no to.

Example

What should a tourist do in your city?

They should go to the old town and try the local food. They shouldn't drive in the centre, because the traffic is terrible. They can take the bus.

Remember

  1. can / must / should + base verb. No to: I can drive. You must stop. You should rest.
  2. They are the same for every subject. No -s: She can swim, not She cans swim.
  3. Questions: put the modal first. Can you help me? Should I call him? Never Do you can…?
