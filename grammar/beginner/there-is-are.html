There Is / There Are and Prepositions of Place

Introduction

Use there is and there are to say that something is in a place. Use prepositions like under, next to and opposite to say exactly where it is.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use there is and there are to say what is in a place.
  • Make negatives and questions with there is and there are.
  • Say where things are with under, next to, behind, in front of, between, near and opposite.

1. There Is / There Are

Form: there is (there's) + a / an + singular noun / there are + plural noun

When to use it: Use it to say that something exists in a place.

  1. There's a café near my office.
  2. There are two bedrooms in my flat.
  3. There's a good bakery on our street.

2. Negatives and Questions

Form: there isn't / there aren't / is there...? / are there...?

When to use it: Use these to say something is not there, or to ask if it is.

  1. There isn't a lift in my building.
  2. Is there a bank near here?
  3. Are there buses on Sundays?

Short answers: Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. / Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

3. Prepositions of Place

Form: noun + under / next to / behind / in front of / between / near / opposite + place

When to use it: Use these words to say exactly where something is.

  1. There's a supermarket opposite my house.
  2. Your keys are under the newspaper.
  3. The bathroom is next to the kitchen.

More examples: behind the door, in front of the station, between the bank and the café, near my house.

Summary: There Is / There Are and Prepositions of Place

Singular Plural
Positive There's a park. There are two parks.
Negative There isn't a lift. There aren't buses on Sundays.
Question Is there a bank near here? Are there buses on Sundays?
Short answers Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.
Preposition Example
under The bag is under the table.
next to The bank is next to the café.
behind The car park is behind the hotel.
in front of Meet me in front of the station.
between The shop is between the bank and the café.
near I live near the beach.
opposite There's a park opposite my house.
