Comparative Adjectives

Introduction

Use comparative adjectives to compare two people or things: older, cheaper, more expensive. Use than before the second thing.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Make comparatives with -er for short adjectives.
  • Use more with long adjectives.
  • Use than and the irregular forms better and worse.

1. Short Adjectives + -er

Form: adjective + -er + than

When to use it: Use it with short adjectives (one syllable, or two ending in -y).

  1. My brother is older than me.
  2. The train is faster than the bus.
  3. It's colder today.

Spelling: nice → nicer, big → bigger, hot → hotter, easy → easier, busy → busier.

2. Long Adjectives: More

Form: more + adjective + than

When to use it: Use more with longer adjectives.

  1. This hotel is more expensive than the other one.
  2. My new job is more interesting.
  3. For me, Italian is more difficult than Spanish.

3. Irregular Comparatives

Form: good → better, bad → worse, far → further

When to use it: These don't follow the rules. Learn them one by one.

  1. I feel better today.
  2. The traffic is worse on Fridays.
  3. My new office is further from home.

Summary: Comparative Adjectives

Adjective Rule Comparative
old, cheap + er older, cheaper
nice, late + r nicer, later
big, hot double the last letter + er bigger, hotter
easy, busy y → ier easier, busier
expensive, interesting more + adjective more expensive, more interesting
good, bad, far irregular better, worse, further
