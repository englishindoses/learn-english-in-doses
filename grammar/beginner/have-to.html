Have To / Don't Have To

Introduction

Use have to to say something is necessary, often because of a rule or your job. Use don't have to to say something is not necessary.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use have to and has to for things that are necessary.
  • Use don't have to for things that are not necessary.
  • Ask questions with Do you have to...?

1. Have To / Has To

Form: I / you / we / they + have to + base verb / he / she / it + has to + base verb

When to use it: Use it to say something is necessary.

  1. I have to get up at six for work.
  2. She has to wear a uniform.
  3. You have to show your passport at the airport.

2. Don't Have To

Form: don't / doesn't have to + base verb

When to use it: Use it to say something is not necessary. You can do it if you want.

  1. I don't have to work on Saturdays.
  2. You don't have to bring anything.
  3. He doesn't have to wear a suit.

3. Questions

Form: Do / Does + subject + have to + base verb...?

When to use it: Use do or does to ask, like other present simple questions.

  1. Do you have to work tomorrow?
  2. Does she have to travel for her job?
  3. What time do you have to be at the airport?

Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Summary: Have To / Don't Have To

I / you / we / they he / she / it
Positive I have to go. She has to go.
Negative I don't have to go. She doesn't have to go.
Question Do you have to go? Does she have to go?
Short answers Yes, I do. / No, I don't. Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
