This, That and Possessive Adjectives

Introduction

Use this and that to point at things. Use possessive adjectives (my, your, his, her...) to say who something belongs to.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use this for things near you and that for things far from you.
  • Use my, your, his, her, its, our and their before a noun.
  • Ask and answer questions like Is this your bag?

1. This and That

Form: this + singular noun (near) / that + singular noun (far)

When to use it: This is for things here, near you. That is for things over there.

  1. This coffee is really good.
  2. Who's that man over there?
  3. Is this seat free?

2. Possessive Adjectives

Form: my / your / his / her / its / our / their + noun

When to use it: Use these words to say who something belongs to.

  1. My brother lives in Manchester.
  2. What's your name?
  3. They love their new flat.

His or her? Use his for a man and her for a woman: Tom and his wife, Anna and her husband.

3. Putting Them Together

Form: this / that + is + my / your / his... + noun

When to use it: Use this to show things or ask who they belong to.

  1. This is my phone.
  2. Is that your car?
  3. That's her office.

Summary: This, That and Possessive Adjectives

Subject Possessive adjective Example
I my This is my coat.
you your Is this your bag?
he his That's his car.
she her Her name is Anna.
it its The cat loves its bed.
we our Our house is small.
they their That's their dog.

This = near me. That = over there.
