Imperatives

Introduction

Use the imperative to give instructions, directions and advice. It's the base form of the verb, with no subject. You'll hear it everywhere: at work, at home and when you travel.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Give instructions and directions with the base form of the verb.
  • Tell someone not to do something with don't.
  • Make instructions polite with please.

1. Positive Imperatives

Form: base verb + ...

When to use it: Use it to give instructions, directions or good wishes.

  1. Turn left at the bank.
  2. Call me when you get home.
  3. Have a great weekend!

2. Negative Imperatives

Form: don't + base verb + ...

When to use it: Use it to tell someone not to do something.

  1. Don't forget your keys.
  2. Don't worry. It's fine.
  3. Don't park here. It's for residents only.

3. Polite Imperatives

Form: please + imperative / imperative + please

When to use it: Add please to make an instruction polite.

  1. Please take a seat.
  2. Wait here, please.
  3. Please don't smoke in the car.

With friends and family, imperatives are normal. With people you don't know, add please.

Summary: Imperatives

Type Form Example
Positive base verb Open the window.
Negative don't + base verb Don't open the window.
Polite please + imperative Please open the window. / Open the window, please.
