Present Perfect Quiz

This quiz covers the first three topics of the intermediate course: the Present Perfect Simple, the Present Perfect Continuous, and choosing between them. There are four rounds and 31 questions in total.

How it works:

  1. Round 1 — Present Perfect Simple. Choose the correct option in each sentence.
  2. Round 2 — Present Perfect Continuous. Put the words in the correct order.
  3. Round 3 — Simple or Continuous? Click the form that fits the situation.
  4. Round 4 — Mixed review. Ten multiple-choice questions with explanations.

Each round has its own Check Answers button, so you get feedback as you go. You can retry any round as many times as you like.

Quick Reminder Before You Start

Form Structure We use it for Example
Present Perfect Simple have / has + past participle (V3) Experience, finished actions with a result now, how many, how much. I have lost my keys.
Present Perfect Continuous have / has + been + verb-ing How long an activity has been going on, activities that explain a present situation. I have been waiting for an hour.

Remember: state verbs (know, be, have for possession, like, believe) are not normally used in the continuous. Say "I have known her for years," not "I have been knowing her for years."

If You Found This Difficult

📝 TIP 1: Ask yourself "how many?" or "how long?" If the sentence answers how many (two coffees, three emails), use the simple. If it answers how long (for an hour, since Monday, all morning), use the continuous.

📝 TIP 2: Finished time means past simple. "I booked the tickets last night" is finished and over. "I've booked the tickets" says nothing about when — it's the result that matters now.

📝 TIP 3: "Has gone to" means the person is away now. "Has been to" means the person went and came back. Compare: "She's gone to the shops" (she's out) and "She's been to the shops" (she's home with the shopping).
