Advanced Quantifiers

Introduction

Quantifiers are words and phrases that tell us how much or how many of something there is. In this lesson, we’ll look at advanced quantifiers used with uncountable and abstract nouns. We often use them in formal writing and discussions about ideas and feelings.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Describe large and small amounts with uncountable and abstract nouns.
  • Use natural combinations of quantifiers and nouns.
  • Choose quantifiers to emphasise an amount, make a comparison or leave the amount unspecified.

1. Quantifiers and Their Meanings

Form: quantifier + uncountable noun.

When to use it: Use these quantifiers before uncountable nouns. They show roughly how much, not exact amounts.

From large amounts to almost none:

  • A great deal of: a large amount.
  • A considerable amount of: a large amount; more formal.
  • A certain amount of: some, without saying how much.
  • Relatively little: a small amount compared with something else.
  • Virtually no: almost none.
  1. The new project needs a great deal of work.
    (= There is a lot of work to do.)
  2. Learning to cook takes a certain amount of patience.
    (= You need some patience.)
  3. There was virtually no traffic on the way to the airport.
    (= There were almost no cars on the road.)

Native speaker note: In everyday conversation, native speakers usually say a lot of rather than a considerable amount of, which is more common in reports and formal emails.

2. Common, Natural Collocations

Form: verb + quantifier + uncountable noun.

When to use it: Use these combinations to talk about work, feelings and everyday situations. A collocation is a combination of words often used together.

QuantifierCommon nouns
A great deal of.Effort, attention, support, trouble.
A considerable amount of.Time, money, work, research.
A certain amount of.Patience, confidence, flexibility, risk.
Relatively little.Experience, information, space, effort.
Virtually no.Progress, interest, evidence, traffic.
  1. The team put a great deal of effort into the report.
    (= They worked hard on it.)
  2. The repairs cost a considerable amount of money.
    (= The repairs were expensive.)
  3. The airline allows a certain amount of flexibility with dates.
    (= Some changes to travel dates are possible.)

Native speaker note: Native speakers say make progress and do research. The verb stays the same when a quantifier is added: make virtually no progress, do a considerable amount of research.

3. Nuance: Choosing the Right Quantifier

Form: quantifier + uncountable noun.

When to use it: Choose a quantifier to show more than just the amount. Your choice can add emphasis, make a comparison or avoid saying exactly how much.

  • A great deal of / a considerable amount of: both mean a large amount. The first often adds emphasis; the second sounds more formal. Neither always means more.
  • A certain amount of: leaves the amount unspecified. It does not necessarily mean a small amount.
  • Relatively little / virtually no: the first compares a small amount with something else. The second stresses that there is almost none, though there may be a tiny amount.
  1. The project took a great deal of time / a considerable amount of time.
    (= Both mean a lot of time. The second sounds more formal.)
  2. There's a certain amount of tension at home.
    (= There is some tension, but we haven't said how much.)
  3. The hotel made relatively little progress / virtually no progress with the repairs.
    (= The first means little progress compared with what was expected. The second means almost none.)

Native speaker note: Native speakers sometimes use a certain amount of concern to raise a problem gently. It sounds less direct than a great deal of concern, but it does not tell you exactly how worried they are.

Summary: Advanced Quantifiers

FormMeaningExample
a great deal of + uncountable noun.A large amount; often adds emphasis.The job takes a great deal of effort.
a considerable amount of + uncountable noun.A large amount; more formal.We spent a considerable amount of money.
a certain amount of + uncountable noun.Some, without saying how much; not necessarily a small amount.The job allows a certain amount of freedom.
relatively little + uncountable noun.A small amount compared with something else. No of before the noun.This flat needs relatively little heating compared with our old one.
virtually no + uncountable noun.Almost none; a tiny amount may remain. No of before the noun.There was virtually no noise.

Common Errors

1: Omitting of after amount

❌ The survey produced a considerable amount data.

✓ The survey produced a considerable amount of data.

Use of to connect amount to the noun.

2: Adding of after relatively little

❌ The new device uses relatively little of power.

✓ The new device uses relatively little power.

Put relatively little directly before the noun, without of.

Useful Tips

📌 TIP 1: Record a quantifier together with two or three nouns from real reports or conversations. Write a new sentence for each combination rather than memorising the phrase alone.

📌 TIP 2: Compare two things you know, such as your current home and your old home. Write one sentence about something that needs relatively little time, money or space.
