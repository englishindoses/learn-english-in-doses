Unit 1 Review

Airport and Airplane — Everything You Have Learned

Review Goal

Check that you can travel through an airport in English, from check-in to baggage claim.

In this review you will:

  • Revise the grammar from Lessons 1 to 5.
  • Practise vocabulary from every part of the airport.
  • Choose the right thing to say when airport staff talk to you.
  • Practise the whole journey in a role play with your teacher.

Grammar Review

Read these examples before you start the activities. The important words are highlighted.

1. Polite Requests

Form: I'd like to + verb  |  Can I have + noun?

  1. I'd like to check in, please.
  2. I'd like to sit by the window.
  3. Can I have a blanket, please?
  4. Can I have a claim form?

2. Questions with Where and What

Form: Where is / are + noun?  |  What time do / does + subject + verb?

  1. Where is gate 15?
  2. Where are the bathrooms?
  3. Where can I put my bag?
  4. What time do we land?
  5. What time is boarding?

Remember: Use is for one thing (the gate, my seat). Use are for more than one thing (the bathrooms, the drink options).

3. Present Continuous for Plans

Form: subject + am / is / are + verb-ing

  1. I'm staying for two weeks.
  2. I'm staying at the Park Hotel.
  3. I'm visiting my sister.

Also remember: Use the present simple for facts about you: "I'm here for tourism." / "I'm a teacher." / "I'm an engineer."

4. Past Simple Negative

Form: subject + didn't + verb (base form)

  1. My bag didn't arrive.
  2. I didn't get my luggage.
  3. The airline didn't call me.

Remember: After didn't, always use the base form. Say "didn't arrive" (NOT "didn't arrived").

5. Will for the Future

Form: subject + will + verb  |  Will + subject + verb?  |  subject + won't + verb

  1. We will call you when we find it.
  2. Will you deliver it to my hotel?
  3. When will my bag arrive?
  4. Your bag won't arrive today.

Short answers: "Will you deliver my bag?" — Yes, we will. / No, we won't.

6. Describing Your Bag

Form: It's a + size + colour + type

  1. It's a big blue suitcase with wheels.
  2. It's a small black bag with a red tag.

Reply to Airport Staff

Airport staff say something to you. Choose the best reply.

1. Check-in agent: "Can I see your passport, please?"

A. Here you are.
B. It's a big blue suitcase.
C. I'm here for tourism.

2. Security officer: "Please take off your belt and shoes."

A. Two weeks, please.
B. Okay, no problem.
C. Carousel 5.

3. Check-in agent: "Would you like a window seat or an aisle seat?"

A. At the Grand Hotel.
B. It didn't arrive.
C. A window seat, please.

4. Flight attendant: "Would you like something to drink?"

A. Can I have some water, please?
B. It's in the overhead bin.
C. My bag didn't arrive.

5. Flight attendant: "Please fasten your seatbelt. There is some turbulence."

A. Nothing to declare.
B. Okay, thank you.
C. I'm staying for five days.

6. Immigration officer: "What's the purpose of your visit?"

A. I'm staying at the Park Hotel.
B. For two weeks.
C. I'm here for business.

7. Immigration officer: "How long are you staying?"

A. I'm staying for ten days.
B. I'm a teacher.
C. Yes, here it is.

8. Customs officer: "Do you have anything to declare?"

A. Gate 15.
B. No, nothing to declare.
C. It's a small black bag.

9. Baggage office staff: "Can you describe your bag?"

A. I'm here for tourism.
B. Yes, I have one bag to check in.
C. It's a big blue suitcase with wheels.

10. Baggage office staff: "We will call you when we find your bag."

A. Thank you. Will you deliver it to my hotel?
B. I'd like to check in, please.
C. Yes, a window seat, please.

🎤 Now It's Your Turn to Speak!

You've finished the activities. Now practise the whole airport journey with your teacher or a partner.

Speaking Practice

Open the Role Play Cards and choose a situation. Student A reads the traveller card. Student B reads the staff card. Do not read each other's cards.

The Whole Journey

When you are ready, try the full journey in one role play. You will:

  1. Check in for your flight and choose a seat.
  2. Go through security.
  3. Ask a flight attendant for what you need on the plane.
  4. Answer the immigration officer's questions.
  5. Go through customs.
  6. Report your missing or damaged bag at the baggage office.

Use the Lost Luggage Form for the last part.

Can You Do These Things?

Tick the things you can do in English without help. If you cannot do one, go back to that lesson.

Remember: You do not need perfect grammar. You need people to understand you. Keep your answers short and clear, and always start with "Excuse me" when you ask for help.
