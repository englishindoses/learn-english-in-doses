Modal Verbs: Can, Must and Should

Introduction

The modals can, must and should are used to talk about ability, permission, obligation and give suggestions. We use them every day, so let's look at how to use them correctly in sentences.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Make positive sentences with can, must and should.
  • Make negative sentences with can't, mustn't and shouldn't.
  • Ask and answer questions with can and should.

Warm Up

Answer these questions.

  1. What can you do well?
  2. What must you do every day?
  3. What should people do to stay healthy?

1. Positive Sentences

Form: subject + can / must / should + base verb

When to use it:

canability or permissionI can swim. / You can sit here.
musta ruleYou must stop at a red light.
shouldadvice, a good ideaYou should see a doctor.
  1. She can speak three languages.
  2. You must wear a helmet on the building site.
  3. You should visit the old town.

2. Negative Sentences

Form: subject + modal verb + not + base verb

When to use it:

can'tnot able, or not allowedI can't drive. / You can't smoke here.
mustn'tnot allowed, against the rulesYou mustn't park here.
shouldn'tnot a good ideaYou shouldn't eat so much sugar.
  1. I can't come to the meeting on Friday.
  2. Visitors mustn't take photos inside the museum.
  3. He shouldn't work so late.

3. Questions

Form: (question word) + can / should + subject + base verb?

When to use it:

Can you …?ask about ability, or ask for helpCan you swim? / Can you help me?
Can I …?ask for permissionCan I open the window?
Should I …?ask for adviceShould I call the client?
  1. Can you send me the plans?
  2. Where can I park my car?
  3. What should we do now?

Short answers: Yes, I can. / No, I can't. Yes, you should. / No, you shouldn't.

Summary: Can, Must and Should
