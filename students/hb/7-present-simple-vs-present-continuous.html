Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Introduction

Today we will compare the present simple tense with the present continuous (present progressive) tense.

After this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Choose the right tense to talk about a fact, a habit or an action in progress.
  • Use both tenses to explain what is different from your usual routine today or this week.
  • Ask and answer questions about what people usually do and what they’re doing now.

Present Simple

Form

  • Positive: subject + base verb. Usually add -s with he, she and it.
  • Negative: subject + don’t / doesn’t + base verb
  • Question: do / does + subject + base verb?

With be, use am / is / are. Add not for negatives, or put am / is / are before the subject for questions.

When to use it: Use it for facts and things you do regularly.

  1. She is a baker.
    (= That’s her job.)
  2. He starts work at six every morning.
    (= That’s his routine.)
  3. What time do you finish work on Fridays?
    (= I’m asking about your usual Friday.)

Present Continuous

Form

  • Positive: subject + am / is / are + verb-ing
  • Negative: subject + am / is / are + not + verb-ing
  • Question: am / is / are + subject + verb-ing?

When to use it: Use it for actions happening now or for a short time around now.

  1. She is making bread.
    (= She’s doing it now.)
  2. I’m not driving to work this week.
    (= This week is different from usual.)
  3. What are you working on today?
    (= I’m asking about your work today.)
