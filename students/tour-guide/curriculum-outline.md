# Tour guide English — course outline

Survival level. Enough to run a tour in English, nothing more. Each unit is one
lesson page. Order runs easiest to hardest, and roughly follows the order of a
real tour, so early units are useful on their own before the course is finished.

Status: skeleton. Content and activities get filled in per unit.

---

## 1. Introducing yourself

The first thirty seconds of every tour. Fully scripted, memorised.

- Grammar: *be* in the present — I'm, you're. Present simple questions.
- Content: My name is Vitor. I'm your tour guide today. I'm 31. I'm from
  Cachoeiras. Plus the same three questions asked back to tourists.

## 2. Greeting the group

- Grammar: imperatives for simple instructions — follow me, this way, wait here.
- Content: welcome, please, thank you, excuse me. Where everyone is from.

## 3. Time

- Grammar: numbers, telling the time, *how long*.
- Content: what time we start, how long the walk takes, when we come back,
  how long the stop is. Meeting again at a set time.

## 4. Safety and rules

The unit where bad English is actually dangerous, so it stays short and blunt.

- Grammar: imperatives, positive and negative — be careful, don't touch,
  watch your step, stay on the path, don't put your hands in holes.
- Content: snakes, caterpillars, wet rocks, slippery ground. Group rules —
  stay together, don't litter, don't swim here.

## 5. Nature vocabulary

- Grammar: none new. Plurals.
- Content: river, waterfall, trail, path, rock, tree, forest, bird, snake,
  insect, plant, bridge, pool.

## 6. Describing the place

- Grammar: there is / there are.
- Content: uses unit 5's words. There's a waterfall. There are two rivers.
  Is there a toilet? Answering where things are.

## 7. The parks and protected area

- Grammar: present simple for facts. Can / can't for what's allowed.
- Content: the parks and protected areas around Cachoeiras, why they're
  protected, what visitors can and can't do there.

## 8. History and "the most"

Hardest unit, so it comes last.

- Grammar: past simple of *be*. Superlatives — the most, the biggest.
- Content: short history of Cachoeiras. The hundred rivers. Cachoeiras has
  the most rivers and waterfalls in the state of Rio.
